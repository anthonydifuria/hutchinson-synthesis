## Phase 2 — β sweep (kernel × energy × β, 168 configs)

Data: `phase2_beta_sweep_raw.csv`, `phase2_beta_sweep_closest.csv`.
Plots: `plots/beta_sweep_repulsive.png` (β ≤ 0), `plots/beta_sweep_attractive.png`
(β ≥ 0). Generated by `gigp_explorer/mmd_batch_validation/run_validation.py`.

### Why two plots instead of one

The first version produced a single plot (`beta_sweep_overview.png`, now
removed) with a shared y-axis (0-0.5) across all 8 subplots. Numerical
analysis on the CSV:

- β < 0 (repulsive regime): MMD² max 0.515, mean 0.125
- β ≥ 0 (attractive regime): MMD² max 0.027, mean 0.006

The dynamic range of MMD in the attractive regime is real but ~20 times
smaller than in the repulsive one -- also verified at the code level (the
greedy loop in `_campiona_permanental` always selects all the requested
points, no random fallback diluting the signal). With a shared y-axis, the
real signal on the attractive side appeared visually flattened to zero,
giving a false impression of "no discrimination above β=0". Hence the
choice to split the two regimes into two figures, each with its own
y-scale. No change to the sampling code (`gigp.py`) was needed: the
problem was only in the visualisation, not in the algorithm.

### Open observation, not yet resolved

Looking at the repulsive-regime plot, a sharp discontinuity is visible
exactly at β=0: MMD drops from ~0.35-0.5 (at β=-0.1) to ~0.00-0.02 (at
β=0.0) in a single step, while the rest of the curve (from β=-1.0 to
β=-0.1) changes more gradually. Probable cause identified in the code: at
exactly β=0.0, `campiona_gigp_esatto` takes a completely different branch
(pure uniform random sampling, because the sum of the β weights is zero),
while at β=-0.1 it still uses the DPP-greedy branch (with the kernel
weighted at 10%). DPP-greedy, even with a weak weight, remains a sequential
selection algorithm that maximises diversity, structurally different from
IID uniform sampling -- so the transition between the two branches is not
a smooth one. Not yet discussed with Anthony, to be investigated further
before using the repulsive side of the plot in any "continuous
interpolation" narrative in the paper.

### Second observation (confirmed with direct tests): the Gibbs correction is too weak

Anthony noticed that in the repulsive plot the 4 energy panels (none,
strauss, hawkes, both) look almost identical for a given kernel. It is not
an MMD normalisation issue -- it is an upstream problem, in the Gibbs
corrector (`_metropolis_gibbs`, Block 3).

Direct test: 50 points selected via pure DPP-greedy (β=-1, Gaussian
kernel), then Strauss applied (w_strauss=3.0, R_strauss=0.08):

| n_passi | energy H | Strauss violations | points actually moved |
|---------|-----------|---------------------|------------------------------|
| 0 (start) | 534.0 | 178 | -- |
| 5 (current default) | 531.0 | 177 | 1 / 50 |
| 20 | 507.0 | 169 | 11 / 50 |
| 50 | 465.0 | 155 | 23 / 50 |
| 200 | 378.0 | 126 | 42 / 50 |
| 1000 | 294.0 | 98 | 50 / 50 |

With `n_passi=5` (the default value fixed throughout the code, in both
`gigp_explorer/gigp.py` and `hutchinson_pure/gigp.py`, identical) the
Metropolis-Gibbs corrector effectively moves **1 point out of 50**.
Consistent with the near-zero MMD measured between energy=none and
energy=strauss/hawkes/both for the same beta and kernel (0.0001-0.005
across the whole tested β range) -- the signal Anthony noticed "by eye" in
the plots is real and numerically confirmed, not a plotting artefact.

Note: R_strauss=0.08 over 50 points in [0,1] is a very tight constraint
regardless (theoretical maximum of non-overlapping points ≈ 1/0.08 ≈ 12),
so even a perfectly converged sampler would not reach zero violations with
a finite weight w_strauss=3.0 (a true Strauss process discourages nearby
pairs, it does not forbid them) -- but the jump from 178 to 177 violations
in 5 steps is still far from any notion of equilibrium, even approximate.

Implication: this is not just about the validation script -- the same
`_metropolis_gibbs` with `n_passi=5` is used by the actual synthesis
engine (`hutchinson_pure/gigp.py`, identical to `gigp_explorer/gigp.py`),
so any niche configured with Strauss or Hawkes in the real system probably
does not substantially respect the energy constraint. No code change
applied yet -- pending, to be discussed with Anthony before deciding
whether to increase `n_passi`, redesign the correction, or document the
limitation in the paper as it stands.

### Hawkes fix: numerical minimum-distance constraint

`_metropolis_gibbs` now rejects any move that would bring two points below
`eps_floor = 0.1/k` on a dimension with active energy (Strauss or Hawkes),
regardless of the energy value. It does not touch eq. 13 of the paper (the
Hawkes formula remains exactly the published one) -- it is a safeguard at
the sampler level only. Applied identically to `gigp_explorer/gigp.py` and
`hutchinson_pure/gigp.py` (verified identical after the change).

Verification: same Hawkes-only configuration as the previous test (β=0,
w_hawkes=2.0, decay=5.0, k=50) -- before the fix the points collapsed from
std=0.282 to std=0.028 (almost coincident); after the fix they reach
std=0.051, minimum distance between points = 0.00206 (>= eps_floor=0.002
as expected) -- strong clustering but not degenerate.

### Plot fix: a single graph, symlog y-axis

`plot_overview` now produces a single figure again
(`beta_sweep_overview.png`, 2x4 panels, continuous β axis from -1 to 1)
instead of two separate images with different scales. The y-axis uses a
symlog scale (linear near zero, logarithmic beyond `linthresh=1e-3`)
instead of linear: the data spans several orders of magnitude
(0.0003 - 0.5) and includes exact zeros at the self-identification points,
which a plain log scale could not handle. Rationale: the two-image version
with different scales (v2) had the same visual-honesty problem it was
meant to solve in v1's shared scale, just the other way round -- two
panels with silently different axes can give the impression of comparable
magnitudes. A single graph with a single, explicitly non-linear axis
avoids both problems.

### Fix for the plateau/discontinuity at beta=0 in _dpp_greedy_esatto

Precisely diagnosed the issue flagged above ("sharp drop at beta=0"): for
beta in [-0.25, 0) the output of `_dpp_greedy_esatto` was *bit-identical*
for a given candidate pool -- the greedy MAP selection (argmax of
det(K_sub) at each step) is insensitive to the magnitude of
`forza=abs(beta)` until it crosses a threshold sufficient to flip the
"winning" candidate at a given step. On top of this there is a
discontinuous jump exactly at beta=0, where the code switches branch
(uniform random selection instead of weighted greedy) instead of
degrading continuously.

Three alternatives evaluated:
1. Replace with `_campiona_dpp_spettrale` (already present in the code,
   standard spectral DPP) -- discarded: at forza=1.0 it produces too few
   points (~4-8 instead of the 50 required), would have required random
   fill-in and degraded the pure DPP case (beta=-1) already published in
   Table I.
2. Probabilistically weighted greedy selection instead of pure argmax --
   discarded: same underlying issue, the informative signal is exhausted
   after ~8 points out of 40-50 candidates.
3. **Per-step "coin-flip" blend** (chosen fix): for each point to select,
   with probability `forza` make today's greedy choice (argmax det), with
   probability `(1-forza)` choose uniformly at random among the remaining
   candidates.

**Implementation**: `_dpp_greedy_esatto(K, n_target, forza=1.0, rng=None)`
in `gigp_explorer/gigp.py` and `hutchinson_pure/gigp.py` (identical,
verified via diff). The single call site (in `campiona_gigp_esatto`,
beta<=0 branch) now passes `forza` and `rng`:
`_dpp_greedy_esatto(L_eff, n, forza, rng)`.

**Guarantee for the already-published case (beta=-1, Table I)**: at
`forza=1.0` the condition `rng.random() >= forza` is always false
(rng.random() ∈ [0,1)), so the greedy branch is taken at every step,
without exception -- mathematically, not just empirically, identical to
the previous behaviour. Also verified with `rng=None` (fallback that
skips the coin-flip entirely): result identical to `forza=1.0` with a real
rng. Table I does NOT need to be regenerated for the DPP row/column.

**Plateau check**: with the same K and the same seed, the selected
sequence now varies with `forza` instead of staying frozen:

```
forza=0.001  sel[:8]=[26, 17, 7, 3, 29, 32, 20, 14]
forza=0.050  sel[:8]=[26, 17, 7, 3, 29, 32, 20, 14]
forza=0.100  sel[:8]=[26, 17, 7, 3, 29, 32, 20, 14]
forza=0.150  sel[:8]=[26, 17, 7, 3, 29, 16, 32, 8]
forza=0.200  sel[:8]=[26, 17, 7, 3, 29, 16, 32, 8]
forza=0.250  sel[:8]=[26, 17, 7, 3, 29, 16, 32, 8]
forza=0.300  sel[:8]=[26, 17, 7, 3, 29, 16, 32, 8]
forza=0.500  sel[:8]=[26, 17, 7, 3, 29, 16, 25, 36]
forza=1.000  sel[:8]=[0, 1, 38, 16, 22, 4, 18, 23]
```

The identical sub-sequences between nearby forza values (e.g. 0.001-0.10)
are normal for a threshold-based stochastic algorithm (with small forza,
almost every coin-flip lands on the random branch, with the same seed) --
they are no longer the symptom of the original bug, which was a
*structural freeze* of the greedy branch itself, independent of any seed
or amount of noise.

**Note on reproducibility and seed**: before the fix, `_dpp_greedy_esatto`
did not consume `rng` at all -- its output depended only on K (so the
diversity observed when changing seed came entirely from the upstream
generation of the candidate pool in `genera_centroidi_gigp_1d`, not from
the selection itself). After the fix, the selection consumes additional
numbers from `rng`, but with a fixed seed (e.g. SEED=42 as used in
`run_validation.py`) the result remains perfectly reproducible run after
run, given the same call order in the pipeline.

This is not a change to the formal model: K and L_eff remain exactly what
is described in the paper (Block 2), consistent with the principle that
data can change but concepts cannot. It is a correction to the sampling
algorithm, the same distinction already used for the Block 3 eps_floor.

### Novelty of intermediate β-ensembles: how much are they really "something else"?

Question: when β is continuous (not one of the 8 base presets), does the
sampler produce configurations genuinely different from the 8 reference
distributions, or could they in practice coincide with one of them within
sampling noise? Relevant because the paper explicitly states that
"β∈[-1,+1] navigates continuously between DPP (β=-1), Poisson (β=0), and
Permanental (β=+1)" and that "intermediate values generate all degrees of
structure possible between the two extremes" (Section IV, Block 2) -- a
continuity claim already published and worth checking numerically, not
just qualitatively.

**Method**: the same Phase 2 sweep (168 configurations: 21 betas x 4
energy presets x 2 kernels) was independently regenerated for 12 different
seeds (42, 7, 100-109), using the new `--seed` argument of
`run_validation.py` (one file per seed, without touching the canonical
results -- see previous section). For each configuration, across all 12
seeds: mean and SEM (standard error of the mean) of the MMD to each of the
8 reference distributions.

**Test 1 -- "is it really different from all 8?"** (magnitude of the
distance to the nearest of the 8, relative to its SEM): median ratio 4-6,
with 14-16 configurations out of 16 per |beta| bucket exceeding a ratio of
3 (a conventional threshold for "clearly real, not noise"). Result:
**yes, in a statistically solid way across almost the entire tested beta
range**. The distance to the nearest of the 8 reference distributions was
never found to be compatible with zero (except, obviously, at the anchor
points beta=-1/0/+1, where the distance to itself is 0 by construction).

**Test 2 -- "which of the 8 does it resemble most?"** (difference between
the first- and second-nearest, relative to the combined SEM): much lower
median ratio, 1.3-2.9, with only 3-7 configurations out of 16 per bucket
above the threshold of 3. Example (energy=none, kernel=gaussian): DPP and
Strauss remain nearly tied for most of the beta<0 side (gap/SEM typically
<1), same for Matérn/LGCP/Permanental on the beta>0 side. Result: **the
identity of "which of the 8"** remains often ambiguous, even with 12
independent replicates.

**Interpretation**: the two tests together trace exactly the shape of a
continuum, not a hidden ninth category nor a noisy copy of one of the 8 --
the intermediate configurations are measurably distinct from each of the
8 (Test 1), but without a sharp identity towards a single neighbour
(Test 2), because they genuinely sit "in between" two adjacent regimes.
Consistent with what the paper itself states about the beta-ensembles:
"a formally consolidated denomination for the continuum of intermediate
values has not been identified by the authors in the consulted
literature" (Sec. IV). The data quantitatively confirm the continuity
already asserted in the text, without however supporting a finer label
than the one the paper itself already refrained from promising.

**Recommended use for the paper**: only Test 1 (distance to the nearest of
the 8, mean ± SEM over 12 seeds) is solid enough to be cited numerically.
Test 2 (which of the 8 is nearest) should not be presented as a precise
quantitative result.
